How to verify an OEKO-TEX certificate before you order

A step-by-step certificate check for apparel buyers, plus how the same approach applies to GOTS, GRS and social audits.

Written by the 2D Creation sourcing team in Tirupur, India, who coordinate product development, sampling, production follow-up and quality control for international apparel buyers.

A certificate logo on a supplier profile proves very little. What matters is which company holds the certificate, which products it covers and whether it is still valid on the date you order. This guide walks through a practical OEKO-TEX certificate check so you can verify a Tirupur supplier's claim before you place an order, and explains how the same approach applies to GOTS, GRS and social-compliance audits.

Key takeaways

  • A logo is not proof. Verify the certificate with the issuing body.
  • Check the holder's name and address, the scope, the product class and the expiry date.
  • Make sure the certificate covers the product you are buying, not a different article.
  • Keep a dated copy of every verification on file.

What OEKO-TEX actually certifies

LabelWhat it covers
Standard 100The finished textile product is tested for harmful substances
Made in GreenStandard 100 testing plus traceable, responsible production
STePSustainable production facilities and processes
Leather Standard / ECO PassportOther product and chemical-input certifications

Most apparel buyers meet Standard 100 first. It is divided into product classes, and Class I applies to babies and toddlers up to 36 months and has the strictest limits.

A certificate logo is not proof

Buyers often see logos for OEKO-TEX, GOTS, BSCI, SMETA, WRAP or GRS on a supplier's profile. A logo on its own does not show which facility is certified, for which products, or whether the certificate is still valid.

Five details to check on every certificate

  1. Certificate number and testing institute.
  2. Exact company name and address, matching the factory you are ordering from.
  3. Scope: which products, articles or processes are covered.
  4. Issue and expiry dates: valid on the date of order and production.
  5. Verification: confirm through the issuing body's online tool, not only through the PDF.

How to verify an OEKO-TEX certificate online

  1. Open the OEKO-TEX Label Check tool on the official website.
  2. Enter the certificate number and the issuing institute shown on the label or certificate.
  3. Compare the result with the supplier: company, products, class and validity.
  4. Save a dated screenshot or PDF with your order records.

GOTS keeps a public database of certified companies, which you can search by name. The same principle applies: confirm with the standard owner, not with the supplier.

Certificates are factory-specific, so we verify each factory's current certificate, including holder, scope and validity, before confirming an order.

Common problems we see

  • The certificate belongs to a fabric mill, not the garment factory.
  • The certificate covers a different product or a different class.
  • The certificate has expired or is about to expire during production.
  • The company name differs slightly from the factory's trading name.

Why the agent is not the certificate holder

2D Creation is a sourcing agent, not the certificate holder. Frequently asked questions

How do I verify an OEKO-TEX certificate?

Use the Label Check tool on the OEKO-TEX website and enter the certificate number and the testing institute shown on the label. Confirm that the company name, product scope and validity date match the supplier you are buying from.

What is the difference between OEKO-TEX Standard 100 and Made in Green?

Standard 100 tests the textile product for harmful substances. Made in Green adds traceability and requires that the product is made in environmentally friendly and socially responsible facilities, so it covers more than the final product test.

What does OEKO-TEX product class I mean?

Class I is for products for babies and toddlers up to 36 months and has the strictest limits. Other classes cover products with skin contact, products with little skin contact and decoration or furnishing material.

Is an OEKO-TEX certificate the same as a GOTS certificate?

No. OEKO-TEX Standard 100 tests finished textiles for harmful substances, while GOTS certifies the organic status of fibres across the supply chain, including environmental and social criteria. Some products carry both.

Can a sourcing agent hold the certificate?

Typically no. The certificate belongs to the certified manufacturer or supplier for a defined scope. An agent should show you the factory's certificate and help verify it.
