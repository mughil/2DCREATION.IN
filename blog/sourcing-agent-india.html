Sourcing agent in India: what apparel buyers should know

Which Indian regions make which garments, how an export order runs, and how to verify an agent before you pay.

Written by the 2D Creation sourcing team in Tirupur, India, who coordinate product development, sampling, production follow-up and quality control for international apparel buyers.

"Sourcing agent India" covers a wide range of businesses, from one-person middlemen to large buying houses with their own inspection teams. The right choice depends on what you buy, where it is made and how much support you need. This guide takes the India-level view: how the apparel industry is organised, which clusters make what, how export works, and how to check that an agent is real before you send money.

Key takeaways

  • India's apparel industry is clustered by product. Match your product to the right region before choosing an agent.
  • Check the agent's registration, factories and references, not just the website.
  • Understand the quotation: the Incoterm, the payment terms and what is included.
  • Pay against milestones and inspection, not entirely up front.

How exporting from India works

  1. Quotation and Incoterm. Most apparel quotations are FOB, with the buyer arranging main freight.
  2. Sampling and approval. Samples are approved in writing before bulk.
  3. Production and inspection. Inline and final checks against an agreed standard.
  4. Export documents. Commercial invoice, packing list and shipping bill, handled with a freight forwarder.
  5. Shipment. Sea or air freight to your destination, with import clearance on your side.

How to check that an agent is legitimate

  • Registration: ask for the company registration and GST details and check them on the official portals.
  • Factories: ask which factories will make your product and whether you can visit or video-call them.
  • References: ask for two buyers in your market who will speak to you.
  • Terms: insist on a written quotation, a sample approval step and milestone payments.
  • Red flag: any request for the full order value up front, with no contract.

When to use an agent and when to go direct

Large, stable programmes with in-house technical and quality teams can work well directly with a factory, especially when the buyer can visit regularly. Newer or growing brands, multi-category ranges and buyers without a local team often benefit from one point of coordination that can match each product to a suitable factory and follow production on the ground.

Questions to ask any sourcing partner

Ask how factories are selected, how certificates are verified, what the sampling and approval process is, how quality is inspected and how problems are escalated. Transparent answers matter more than promises. Read how we handle verification in the OEKO-TEX certificate check guide.

Frequently asked questions

Which Indian cities are best for apparel sourcing?

Tirupur is known for knitwear such as T-shirts, hoodies and kidswear. Other clusters specialise in other products, for example Ludhiana for winter knitwear, the Delhi NCR region for woven garments, and Bengaluru for woven shirts and fashion. Match the product to the cluster.

Do I need to visit India before placing an order?

It helps for a first large programme but is not mandatory. A reliable agent can arrange factory profiles, video walk-throughs and inspection reports, and you can visit later once the relationship is established.

What payment terms are normal for apparel exports from India?

Terms vary by buyer, order size and relationship. Common structures include an advance with the balance against shipping documents, or a letter of credit for larger orders. Agree terms in the written quotation.

What does FOB mean in an Indian apparel quotation?

FOB, free on board, means the price includes delivery of the goods to the port of loading in India and the export clearance. You pay for the main sea or air freight and the import costs. Confirm the exact Incoterms in the quotation.

How do I know an agent is legitimate?

Ask for the company registration and GST details, factory names and visits, written terms and references from other buyers. Be cautious of anyone who asks for a full payment up front without a contract.
