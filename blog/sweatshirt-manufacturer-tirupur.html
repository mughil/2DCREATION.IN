Choosing a sweatshirt and hoodie manufacturer in Tirupur

Fleece types, weights, trims and quality checks for hoodie programmes, and how to catch problems before the first wash.

Written by the 2D Creation sourcing team in Tirupur, India, who coordinate product development, sampling, production follow-up and quality control for international apparel buyers.

A hoodie looks simple on a mood board and is one of the harder knit garments to get right. Fleece is heavy, trims multiply, print behaves differently on a napped surface, and a fit that works in a medium can fail in an XXL. This guide explains how sweatshirt and hoodie production works in Tirupur, which choices drive cost, and how to avoid the problems that appear only after the first wash.

Key takeaways

  • Choose the fleece type and GSM first. They set the feel, the price and the season.
  • Test shrinkage and pilling on a washed sample before bulk.
  • Approve trims, ribs and drawcords as carefully as the body fabric.
  • Run strike-offs on the real fleece for any print, puff or embroidery.

Choosing the right fleece fabric

FabricFeelTypical use
Brushed fleeceWarm, soft, napped insideWinter hoodies and sweatshirts
French terry (loopback)Lighter, breathable, looped insideSpring, autumn, activewear
Heavyweight fleeceDense, structured, boxy drapeStreetwear, premium basics

Weight is stated in GSM. As a rough guide, light hoodies sit around 240 to 280 GSM, mid-weight around 300 to 350 and heavyweight at 380 and above. Confirm fibre composition, knit structure, GSM and whether the fabric is pre-shrunk before sampling, so that fit and handfeel are judged on the real material. Construction details that change cost

  • Hood: single or double layer, lining, drawcord and eyelets.
  • Pockets: kangaroo, zip or side-seam, and how they are reinforced.
  • Rib: cuffs and hem rib stretch and recovery, which often fail first.
  • Seams: flatlock or overlock, and bar tacks at stress points.

List every trim in the tech pack and approve trim samples together with the garment. For the full range we coordinate, see hoodie and sweatshirt sourcing.

Printing, embroidery and washes

Screen print, puff print, embroidery and garment washes each need their own approval. Fleece pile can make fine detail look soft or blurry, so ask for strike-offs on the actual fabric and check print durability after washing.

Available decoration includes screen printing, digital printing, embroidery, appliqué and heat transfer, subject to fabric, artwork and quantity feasibility.

Fit, sizing and grading

Hoodies are commonly oversized or boxy, which makes grading across sizes more important than the base size. Approve a full size set, and check sleeve length, hood opening and body length at the extremes of the range.

Quality control for fleece programmes

  • Fabric inspection for shade, GSM and pilling before cutting.
  • Shrinkage and colourfastness test on a washed swatch.
  • Inline checks on rib recovery, seam strength and trim attachment.
  • Final inspection against an agreed AQL standard before packing.

See quality control and production follow-up.

Cost drivers and timeline

Indicative cost for a hoodie is USD 8 to 18 per piece. Final price depends on fabric, GSM, construction, trims, printing or embroidery, quantity, packaging and testing. Sampling takes 1 to 2 weeks, and the total from approval to shipment is about 8 to 10 weeks.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between brushed fleece and French terry?

Brushed fleece has a soft, napped inside and is warmer and heavier. French terry has small loops on the inside, is lighter and more breathable, and suits spring and autumn. Many brands use loopback for lighter hoodies and brushed fleece for winter.

What GSM is right for a hoodie?

Light hoodies are often 240 to 280 GSM, mid-weight 300 to 350 GSM and heavyweight 380 GSM and above. Confirm the weight on a swatch, because the same GSM can feel very different depending on yarn and finish.

How do I reduce pilling and shrinkage?

Choose combed yarn, ask for the fabric to be pre-shrunk or compacted, and specify a shrinkage tolerance. Test a washed sample before bulk production.

What is the minimum order quantity for hoodies?

Our typical MOQ is 300 pieces per style and colour. Custom-dyed fleece usually needs higher quantities than stock colours.

Can you do embroidery and puff print on fleece?

Yes. Fleece has a thick pile, so we recommend a strike-off on the real fabric to check how the print or stitch sits, and a wash test before bulk.
