Kidswear safety requirements: a buyer checklist for sourcing

Kidswear safety checklist for apparel buyers: cords, small parts, chemicals, labelling, certificates and testing, and what to agree before development starts.

Written by the 2D Creation sourcing team in Tirupur, India, who coordinate product development, sampling, production follow-up and quality control for international apparel buyers.

Safety requirements for children’s clothing depend on the destination market, the age group and the product. This guide is a buyer checklist, not legal advice. It explains the areas that are usually covered, so that you can brief a factory and agree testing before development starts. Always confirm the exact requirements for your market with your testing laboratory or compliance adviser.

Key takeaways

  • Requirements differ by market and age group. The buyer supplies them, and they are agreed before sampling.
  • Cords, drawstrings and small parts are design decisions, so settle them at the design stage.
  • Chemical, labelling and flammability rules can apply, and children’s sleepwear is often treated separately.
  • Certificates belong to the factory. Check the certificate, its scope and its expiry date.

Areas to cover in the brief

Kidswear safety areas to cover in the brief
Age groupState the intended age range and size chart. Rules are often stricter for babies and toddlers.
Cords and drawstringsMany markets restrict cords and drawstrings on children’s clothing, particularly around the neck and hood. In Europe, EN 14682 is the commonly referenced standard. Confirm the rule for your market before designing a hood or waist cord.
Small parts and trimsButtons, snaps, appliqué and decorations can come loose. Agree attachment strength testing and avoid detachable parts for the youngest ages.
ChemicalsMarkets restrict certain substances in textiles and in children’s products. Agree which restricted-substance list and test reports you require.
FlammabilitySome markets have flammability rules, and children’s sleepwear commonly has its own. Check whether your product category is covered.
LabellingFibre content, care instructions, size, country of origin and any age or warning labels. Rules differ by destination.

Fabric and comfort

Safety also includes comfort. Soft fabric, flat seams, tagless labels and no scratchy trims matter for children and especially for babies. Specify them in the tech pack so the sample reflects them. For clothing from 0 to 24 months, see how to choose a babywear manufacturer in India.

Certificates and testing

A certificate such as OEKO-TEX applies to a specific product, scope and period, and it is held by the certified factory or supplier, not by a sourcing agent. Before you rely on one, check the certificate number, the certified products and the expiry date with the issuer. Our guide to verifying an OEKO-TEX certificate explains the steps. A certificate does not replace testing on your own product when your market requires it.

A practical order of work

  1. Confirm the destination market and age group.
  2. List the applicable rules and tests, and agree who pays for them.
  3. Design to those rules: trims, cords, labels and fabric.
  4. Test the sample or a pre-production sample, not only the first prototype.
  5. Check labels and packing on the pre-production sample.
  6. Include the safety points in production quality control.

Frequently asked questions

Who decides which safety standards apply?

The buyer, based on the destination market and product. A testing laboratory or compliance adviser can confirm the exact rules. The factory is then briefed against them.

Does 2D Creation hold safety certificates?

No. 2D Creation is a sourcing agent. Certificates belong to the chosen factory, and the certificate, scope and expiry should be checked before an order is confirmed.

Can a hoodie for children have a drawstring?

Many markets restrict drawstrings and cords on children’s clothing, especially at the neck and hood. Check the rule for your market and age group before you design it.
