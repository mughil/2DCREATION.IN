Kidswear sourcing in Tirupur: fabric, safety rules and sampling

How to brief, test and approve children's clothing in Tirupur so the product that ships is the one you signed off.

Written by the 2D Creation sourcing team in Tirupur, India, who coordinate product development, sampling, production follow-up and quality control for international apparel buyers.

Tirupur is India's knitwear centre, and a large share of the world's cotton kidswear starts here. But kidswear is the category where a vague brief costs the most. A T-shirt for an adult can tolerate a loose neck or a slightly different print. A garment for a two-year-old cannot tolerate a loose snap, a stiff seam or a failed chemical test. This guide covers how to brief, sample and approve kidswear in Tirupur so that the product that ships is the product you signed off.

Key takeaways

  • Kidswear needs a stricter brief than adult wear: fabric softness, trim safety, size grading and destination-market compliance all have to be specified up front.
  • Approve a full size set, not only the base size.
  • Decide on the certification (GOTS, OEKO-TEX) before fabric is chosen, not after.
  • Test trims and prints against your destination market's rules before bulk production starts.

Why Tirupur for kidswear

Tirupur has the full chain close together: yarn, knitting, dyeing, printing, embroidery and stitching. For kidswear this matters because small, soft garments depend on good knit quality and consistent dye lots, and a short supply chain means fewer handoffs where quality can slip. It is also easier to switch fabric or trim mid-sampling when the suppliers are in the same city.

Fabric and certification

Most kidswear programmes start with single jersey, rib, interlock or fleece in combed cotton. Choose the fabric weight first, then the standard. If you need GOTS for organic fibre or OEKO-TEX Standard 100 for tested textiles, state it in the brief, because the whole supply chain, from yarn to dyeing, has to be certified for the claim to hold. 2D Creation coordinates factory selection against these standards and verifies each factory's certificate copy, scope and validity before order confirmation.

Certificates are factory-specific, so we verify each factory's current certificate, including holder, scope and validity, before confirming an order.

Trims, prints and small parts

Anything a child can pull or chew is a safety risk. Snaps, buttons, zips and appliqués need pull testing and secure attachment. Printing inks and embellishments should meet the rules of your destination market. List every trim and finish in the tech pack, with supplier and test report where possible.

  • Drawstrings and cords: restricted on children's upper garments in many markets, so check the rule before designing them in.
  • Prints: water-based or discharge prints feel softer, and are easier to certify, than heavy plastisol on baby garments.
  • Labels: tagless heat-transfer labels avoid itching on newborn skin; fibre content and care labels still have to meet destination rules.

Know your destination market's rules

Kidswear compliance is market-specific. The United States applies CPSIA to children's products, including testing and tracking labels. The European Union applies REACH chemical restrictions and EN 14682 for cords and drawstrings on children's clothing. Other markets have their own rules, so confirm yours with an accredited testing lab and tell us the destination early.

Size sets and fit approval

Kidswear ranges span many sizes, and a pattern that fits the base size can fail at the ends. Approve a full size set, then check grading, neck openings and snap placement in each. Keep the approved set as the physical reference for production, and measure the first bulk pieces against it before cutting continues.

Cost and timeline

Indicative cost for kidswear is USD 3 to 8 per piece. Final price depends on fabric, GSM, construction, trims, printing or embroidery, quantity, packaging and testing. Sampling takes 1 to 2 weeks, bulk fabric 3 to 4 weeks and production 4 to 6 weeks.

Kidswear sourcing checklist

  1. Define age group, season and destination market.
  2. Choose fabric, weight and certification.
  3. Write the tech pack: measurements for every size, trims, print and label details.
  4. Approve the first sample on fit, hand-feel and construction.
  5. Approve a full size set and a trim and print test.
  6. Agree the inspection standard before production starts.
  7. Run an inline check and a final inspection before shipment.

Frequently asked questions

Can 2D Creation source organic cotton kidswear?

Yes. We coordinate factories that work to organic standards such as GOTS. Certificate copies, scope and validity are verified per factory before order confirmation, and we recommend stating the standard at briefing stage.

What should a kidswear tech pack include?

Measurements for every size in the range, fabric composition and GSM, a full trim list, print and embroidery details, labelling and country-of-origin requirements, and packing instructions.

What safety rules apply to children's clothing?

It depends on the market. The US applies CPSIA to children's products, and the EU applies REACH chemical limits and EN 14682 on cords and drawstrings. Confirm the rules for your own market with a testing lab, and tell us the destination so compliance is planned from the start.

What is the minimum order quantity for kidswear?

It depends on fabric and construction. Our typical MOQ is 300 pieces per style and colour. Because kidswear spans many sizes, MOQ is usually quoted per style and colour across the size run.

How long does a kidswear order take?

Typical lead times are 1 to 2 weeks for sampling, 3 to 4 weeks for bulk fabric and 4 to 6 weeks for production, which is about 8 to 10 weeks in total from approval to shipment. Prints, appliqués and organic fabric usually add time, so we confirm a schedule after reviewing the brief.
