Kidswear requirements

Make safety and comfort explicit.

Requirements vary by product, age group and destination market. Buyers should provide their applicable standards, testing protocols and restricted design or trim requirements before development.

  • Intended age group, size range and approved measurement chart
  • Comfort, movement, coverage and skin-contact considerations
  • Buyer rules for cords, drawstrings, small parts and attachments
  • Applicable chemical, flammability, labelling and testing requirements
  • Seam, trim, decoration and workmanship acceptance criteria
  • Care instructions, warnings and destination-market information

Common questions

Womenswear and kidswear basics.

Can women’s and kids’ styles share the same development process?

The broad stages are similar, but measurements, fit, comfort, construction, trims and applicable market requirements must be defined separately for each product and customer group.

What should a kidswear enquiry include?

Include the intended age group, size range, product, materials, quantity, destination market, design details and all applicable buyer safety, testing and labelling requirements.

How are prints coordinated across different sizes?

Artwork scale, placement, repeat and panel position should be reviewed against the size range and garment construction before final approval.

Is 2D Creation a kidswear manufacturer?

No. 2D Creation is a sourcing agent. It matches your kidswear or womenswear brief to a manufacturer in India and coordinates sampling, production follow-up, quality control and shipment readiness.

Does 2D Creation hold safety certificates for children’s clothing?

No. Certificates belong to the chosen factory. Buyers supply the standards that apply in the destination market, and the certificate, scope and validity should be checked before an order is confirmed.

Can you source babywear?

Babywear can be briefed as a separate category with its own fabric, trim, labelling and testing requirements. Read the babywear manufacturer guide before you send a brief.

Range and fit planning

Give every size range its own brief.

A shared colour story does not make two garments the same development task. A women’s lounge set, a dress and a child’s coordinated outfit need separate measurements, fit comments and component approvals. Start with a style list showing the wearer, sizes, quantities and references for each item.

Give every size range its own brief.
Women’s fit reviewIdentify the base size and intended silhouette, then review the relevant body lengths, openings, coverage and movement on the sample. For a set, assess both pieces together and separately. Record which measurements should change before the next fitting.
Kids’ size planningProvide the intended age group together with a measurement chart; age labels alone do not define the garment dimensions. Identify the sizes to be reviewed and record comments for each sampled size rather than carrying one base-size comment across the whole range without review.
Print placementSupply artwork dimensions and placement references for the size range. Review how the design sits on the actual garment panels, particularly where seams, openings or gathered areas affect the visible artwork. Confirm whether placement or artwork scale changes between sizes.
Coordinated componentsList the fabric, colour, trims and labels for each piece in a set. Keep buyer requirements linked to the individual style, intended wearer and destination. Confirm the applicable design and testing requirements before approving components for production.

A practical range handover includes a style list, size breakdown, approved measurements, material references and consolidated sample comments. Bring unresolved fit or artwork decisions into development and sampling before finalising the bulk production brief.

Know your market

Children's clothing rules depend on the destination.

Check the rules for your own market with an accredited testing lab, and tell us the destination at briefing so tests are scheduled before bulk.

United StatesChildren's products fall under CPSIA, which covers testing and tracking labels.
European UnionREACH limits chemical substances, and EN 14682 covers cords and drawstrings on children's clothing.
Other marketsRequirements differ. Share the destination early so the right tests and labels are planned.

We ship to the UK, USA, Europe, Australia and other international markets. Required tests are agreed at briefing and arranged for the destination market.

Fit and sizing

Plan the size range, not just the base size.

Womenswear and kidswear both fail at the ends of the size range if grading is not checked.

  • Approve a full size set, not only the base size.
  • Record point-of-measure tolerances for each size.
  • For womenswear, state the fit model or size chart used and the stretch recovery expected.
  • For kidswear, check neck openings, snap placement and length at each age size.
